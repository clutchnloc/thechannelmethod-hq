The 6-short formula

Every long-form video has these 6 moments in it. Script them in on purpose and the shorts are already there when you edit.

Your shorts week
DayPost

Launch week has two videos, so post 2 shorts a day, one from each.

How to pull them fast
  • When you script, write each of the 6 moments so it makes sense on its own. Say the hook as one clean sentence.
  • Pull shorts from the vertical file DualShot records, so there's no reframing.
  • Right after you finish the long-form edit, duplicate the project and cut all 6 shorts in the same CapCut session.
  • Add captions on screen. Most people watch shorts muted.
  • End every caption with: "Full video on my YouTube, link in bio."
  • Schedule all 6 at once so posting takes no time during salon days.

Filming system

Film while you work, so content doesn't add time to appointments. Get three angles at every station, then move on.

Angle 1 · OverheadHands and hair. The technique shot for retwists, interlocking and parting.
Angle 2 · SideYou and the client together. Shows the work and the conversation.
Angle 3 · FrontYou talking to camera. Explain what you're doing and why.

Stations to cover

Consultation chair · styling chair · shampoo bowl · front desk and booking · your walk-in to the salon. Three angles each, 10–20 seconds per angle is plenty.

Cross-posting routine

  • Film with DualShot Recorder so you get horizontal (YouTube) and vertical (Reels, TikTok, Shorts) in one take.
  • Publish the long-form video on YouTube first.
  • Pull 6 shorts from the finished edit using the 6-short formula (Shorts Plan tab). Post one a day to YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and TikTok.
  • Every clip caption points back to the full video. Add your YouTube link to your Linktree.
  • Pin your best-performing Reel to your Instagram profile each month.

Client and stylist consent

Ask before you film, every time. Get a yes on camera or in writing, film hands and hair only for anyone who prefers it, and never show a client's face without permission. We'll cover the full consent scripts in the cohort.

Decisions to make before our review call

1. Just you, or your stylists too?

You asked for the pros and cons. Here they are.

Channel about you
  • + One clear voice, faster to brand
  • + Fits your education and digital-product goal
  • − Every video depends on you
  • − Less variety to film
Featuring your stylists
  • + More content and a real salon story
  • + Doubles as recruiting for open chairs
  • − Needs their consent and schedules
  • − Harder if a stylist leaves

My suggestion: you're the face of the channel. Bring stylists in as cameos and the occasional interview (video 12 in your content bank).

2. Channel intro

No intro, or under 5 seconds. Viewers drop off during long intros.

3. Your batch day

Which salon day is lightest? That becomes your filming day every other week.

4. Gear budget

How much do you want to spend on a mic and a camera? I'll match picks to it.
